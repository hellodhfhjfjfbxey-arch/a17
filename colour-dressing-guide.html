Colour Dressing

A colour wheel is the most useful styling tool there is. Here is how to read it and wear it.

01

Start with neutrals

Black, white, grey, navy, camel and denim are the base of most wardrobes. They go with each other and with almost any colour, so one coloured piece against neutrals is the easiest way to begin.

02

Complementary colours

Colours opposite each other on the wheel, such as blue and orange, make each other look brighter. Use one as the main colour and the other as a small accent: a scarf, a bag or a shoe.

03

Analogous and tonal

  • Analogous: neighbours on the wheel, such as green, teal and blue. Calm and rich.
  • Tonal: one colour in several shades. Elegant and lengthening.
  • Triadic: three evenly spaced colours. Bold; keep one dominant.
Wear colour on purpose. One deliberate choice beats five accidental ones.
04

Warm and cool

Warm colours (red, orange, yellow) feel energetic and come forward; cool colours (blue, green, violet) feel calm and recede. Many people find one group flatters their skin more; try both near your face in daylight.

05

The 60-30-10 rule

A simple proportion for colour: about 60 percent main colour, 30 percent secondary and 10 percent accent. It works for a single outfit, and for planning a whole wardrobe.
